# Getting Started into Cyber Security Career

Getting started in Cyber Security with a focus on Security Analytics, including AI, ML, and Deep Learning, in 2024 can be an exciting and promising career path. Here's a step-by-step guide on how to get started:

1. **Understand the Basics:**
   * Begin by building a strong foundation in cybersecurity fundamentals. Learn about network security, system security, cryptography, and common attack vectors. Online courses, tutorials, and books are great resources for this.
2. **Programming Skills:**
   * Develop programming and scripting skills. Languages like Python are essential for working with cybersecurity tools, data analysis, and machine learning.
3. **Networking Knowledge:**
   * Gain a deep understanding of networking concepts. Familiarize yourself with TCP/IP, DNS, routing, and protocols, as they are crucial in understanding network security.
4. **Operating Systems:**
   * Become proficient in various operating systems, including Windows, Linux, and macOS. Understanding their security features and vulnerabilities is essential.
5. **Cybersecurity Tools:**
   * Learn to use common cybersecurity tools such as Wireshark, Nmap, Metasploit, and IDS/IPS systems. These tools are essential for analyzing network traffic and identifying security issues.
6. **Security Certifications:**
   * Consider pursuing relevant certifications such as CompTIA Security+, Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP), Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH), or Certified Information Security Manager (CISM) to validate your skills.
7. **Machine Learning and AI:**
   * Start learning about machine learning (ML) and artificial intelligence (AI) concepts. Understand how ML algorithms work, their applications in cybersecurity, and their limitations.
8. **Deep Learning:**
   * Delve into deep learning, a subset of ML, which involves neural networks. Learn about deep neural networks (DNNs) and how they are used for tasks like malware detection and intrusion detection.
9. **Data Analysis:**
   * Develop strong data analysis skills. Learn how to collect, clean, and analyze data related to security incidents. Tools like Jupyter notebooks and Pandas in Python can be invaluable.
10. **Security Analytics:**
    * Focus on security analytics, which involves using AI and ML techniques to detect and respond to security threats. Explore how these technologies can be applied to anomaly detection, behavioral analysis, and predictive modeling.
11. **Hands-on Practice:**
    * Gain practical experience by participating in Capture The Flag (CTF) competitions, setting up your lab environment, and conducting security research projects.
12. **Stay Informed:**
    * Stay up-to-date with the latest cybersecurity trends, vulnerabilities, and threats. Follow cybersecurity news, blogs, podcasts, and academic research.
13. **Networking:**
    * Build a professional network in the cybersecurity field. Attend conferences, webinars, and meetups. Connect with professionals and mentors who can guide you in your career.
14. **Ethical Hacking:**
    * Consider becoming an ethical hacker (penetration tester) to apply your skills in a legal and ethical manner. Gain experience identifying and mitigating vulnerabilities.
15. **Continuous Learning:**
    * Cybersecurity is a rapidly evolving field. Continuously update your knowledge and skills by taking online courses, attending workshops, and pursuing advanced certifications.
16. **Job Search:**
    * Look for entry-level positions like Security Analyst, SOC Analyst, or Junior Penetration Tester to gain hands-on experience. Tailor your resume and cover letter to highlight your skills and certifications.
17. **Internships and Internship Programs:**
    * Consider participating in cybersecurity internships or internship programs offered by organizations, government agencies, or cybersecurity firms.
18. **Contribute to Open Source:**
    * Contribute to open-source cybersecurity projects to showcase your skills and build a portfolio.
19. **Be Ethical and Legal:**
    * Always prioritize ethical and legal behavior in your cybersecurity work. Unauthorized hacking or unethical activities can have severe consequences.
20. **Certify and Specialize:**
    * As you gain experience, consider specializing in a specific area of cybersecurity, such as penetration testing, incident response, or threat intelligence. Obtain advanced certifications in your chosen specialization.
