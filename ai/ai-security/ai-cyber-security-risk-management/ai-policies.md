# AI Policies

An AI policy sets the rules for how an organization builds, buys and uses AI. Without one, teams adopt tools on their own and sensitive data ends up in places no one is tracking.

## What a practical AI policy covers

| Area | Questions it answers |
|---|---|
| **Scope & inventory** | Which AI systems and tools are in use? Who owns each one? |
| **Acceptable use** | Which tools are approved? What may employees paste into them? |
| **Data classification** | Which data classes may never go to external AI services? |
| **Procurement & vendor risk** | Does the vendor train on our data? Where is data stored and for how long? |
| **Development standards** | Threat modeling, red teaming and security testing before release |
| **Human oversight** | Which decisions require human review? |
| **Transparency** | When must users be told they are interacting with AI? |
| **Monitoring & incidents** | How are AI failures, abuse and data leaks detected and reported? |
| **Regulatory alignment** | Mapping to the NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001 and applicable laws such as the EU AI Act |

## Starter acceptable-use rules

* Use only approved AI tools with enterprise data-protection terms.
* Never enter credentials, secrets, customer personal data or regulated data into public AI services.
* Review AI-generated code and content before use; you are accountable for it.
* Label AI-generated content where required.
* Report suspected AI misuse or data exposure to the security team.

## Getting started

1. Inventory current AI use, including shadow AI found through proxy and SaaS logs.
2. Classify each use case by risk (data sensitivity × impact of a wrong output).
3. Publish a short acceptable-use policy first, then build out standards.
4. Assign an owner and review the policy at least yearly; the field changes fast.
